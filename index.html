Welcome to the Frontier Wiki

The player encyclopedia for Afterlife: The New Frontier, the Star Trek–inspired space server. Made by Seven.

On Afterlife: The New Frontier you start as a cadet at the Frontier Academy on Earth. After graduating you command a starship, warp across about 320 star systems and beam down to procedurally generated planets to mine, explore alien ruins, fight native creatures and take contracts. As your crew progresses you refit into bigger hulls, buy a starbase and eventually fight for the lawless Outlands.

Everything is server-side. Custom items, furniture and sounds come from the server's resource pack, which loads when you join. There are no crafting tables or furnaces. Everything is made in the Replicator.

New player?Read Getting started, then follow the glowing markers to the Academy. In game, /guide gives you the Frontier Field Manual book.

The game loop

  1. Train. Graduate from the Academy and claim your free Wren Shuttle.
  2. Earn. Take contracts and away missions, mine deposits and sell at the Nexus Station Exchange.
  3. Grow. Follow the campaign to unlock bigger hulls, then refit at the Nexus shipyard.
  4. Settle. Buy a starbase or a station plot, or set up an outpost.
  5. Fight. Pledge to an alliance and take worlds in the Outlands.

Getting started

From Frontier Wiki · your first hour on the server

1. Arrive at the Sol Arrival Port

New players spawn at the Arrival Port, south of Frontier City on Earth (Sol). An arrow on your action bar points the way to the Academy. You'll get the Frontier Field Manual. If you lose it, type /guide.

2. Ride the Warpline into the city

The Warpline is a monorail between the Arrival Port, Frontier City, the three towns and the Wilds. Step onto a glowing lift pad to reach the platform, then pick your destination at the terminal. Every station has a light-blue beacon beam beside its lift pad, so you can always find your way back. See Sol and Frontier City.

3. Train at the Frontier Academy

The Academy is in the center of Frontier City. Talk to the staff in order. There are nine training steps, and the action bar always shows the current one. You get a field kit, learn the tricorder and Replicator, fire at the phaser range, win a combat simulation and graduate. See Frontier Academy.

4. Claim your ship

After graduating, run /ship claim on Earth to commission your free Wren Shuttle, then /ship board to beam aboard. Your first time aboard starts the shipboard orientation: recycle something into matter, replicate from matter, and make your first warp.

5. Go to work

  • Open /jobs for contracts and /missions for away missions.
  • Warp to a nearby planet from the Helm, step into the transporter and beam down.
  • Mine Warp Crystal (amethyst) and Hull Alloy (raw iron block) deposits underground. Crystals fuel your warp drive and alloy pays for hull refits.
  • Sell cargo at the Station Exchange on Nexus Station.
Starter kitNew captains get an O₂ Air Tank, 3 Energy Cells, 40 fuel and 25 credits. Graduation adds 250 credits and a full EVA Suit. Finishing orientation adds 150 credits and 6 Energy Cells.
Before you buildPlanet worlds regenerate with a new seed every 72 hours. Anything you build on a planet is temporary. Build on your ship, a starbase or an outpost.

Shipboard orientation

From Frontier Wiki · a new captain's first lessons aboard

The first time you board your own ship, the computer walks you through three lessons:

  1. Recycle. Put an item into the Replicator's recycler to turn it into matter.
  2. Replicate. Make something from matter.
  3. Warp. Use the Helm to make your first jump.

The reward is 150 credits and 6 Energy Cells.

Earth (Sol) is the hub. It's normal Earth terrain with flat city districts and highways built into it. The Academy is in the middle of Frontier City. Three towns lie about 2,000 blocks out: Port Aurora to the north, Meridian to the east and Havenwood to the west. The Sol Arrival Port, where new players arrive, is about 1,200 blocks south.

The Warpline

The Warpline is a monorail joining everything on Sol. It has the Arrival Port Line, Aurora Line, Meridian Line, Havenwood Line and Wilds Line.

  • Step onto a lift pad to go up to the platform. Pads are marked with a glowing star, a light beam and a sign.
  • Every station has a light-blue beacon beside its lift pad.
  • Trains slow down in cities and run express on open track. You can't take fall or suffocation damage while riding.
  • Riders going straight through transfer between the Arrival Port Line and the Wilds Line automatically.

The Frontier Wilds

A camp in open country, within about 1,600 blocks of the Arrival Port, for mining and logging before you have a ship. Digging isn't allowed in the cities. Take the Wilds Line from the station on the south side of Frontier City. The camp has a green beacon so you can find your way back to the station.

On Earth

  • /ship claim only works on Earth.
  • The Quartermaster (/qm) sells starter gear and only works on Earth.
  • A comm badge emergency recall (sneak and right-click) brings you back to Earth.

Nexus Station

From Frontier Wiki · the trade hub, shipyard and outpost office

Nexus Station is the space port. It's the system called port on the star map, and /ship course port takes you there. It has three decks: the Hangar deck, the Promenade and the Observation deck. Turbolifts connect them.

Services

  • Station Exchange. This is the only place you can sell. Right-click the exchange to open it.
  • Shipyard. Hull refits happen here only, along with ship modules. A rotating hologram projector shows every hull class. Click the emitter pad to cycle through them.
  • Fuel depot. 2 credits per fuel unit.
  • Outpost Office. Buy Outpost Beacons, prefabs and furniture.

Every system on the star map is a planet with its own world. Worlds only load when someone beams down. Each one is 2,000 × 2,000 blocks with a horizon that drops away into space at the edge. You beam down to a random spot within 700 blocks of the center, at least 120 blocks from other crews' landing sites.

Planets resetPlanet worlds regenerate with a new seed every 72 hours. Don't keep anything on a planet.

Danger

Each planet has a danger rating from 0 to 5, shown as stars. Higher danger means tougher creatures, raiders, bosses and loot, and contracts that pay more.

  • Danger 0, havens: about two per sector. No hostile mobs, no raiders, always breathable.
  • Danger 2+: hostile mobs get buffs, and elite creatures appear.
  • Danger 3+: roaming class bosses.
  • Danger 5: PvP is on.

Lower gravity means higher jumps, slower falls and softer landings. Every custom planet also gets its own variation on its class: flatter, rougher, more craters and so on. Danger-0 worlds are always breathable whatever their class.

What's on a planet

  • Deposits underground: amethyst blocks give Warp Crystals and raw iron blocks give Hull Alloy.
  • Ores specific to each class, for example emerald and raw copper on verdant worlds.
  • Caves and winding tunnels, some open to the surface.
  • Alien sites, about 50 per world, and on some classes a lost city. See Alien sites.
  • Structures: wrecks, pods, relays, ruins, probes, monoliths and craters. Crashed ships hold salvage nodes you can break for parts.
  • Exotic samples: surface, deep, life, special and tissue samples drop while you mine and fight on that world. They sell at the Exchange and some contracts ask for them.

Creatures and bosses

From Frontier Wiki · native wildlife

Frontier's own creatures replace vanilla hostile mobs on planets. Sol and other non-planet worlds keep vanilla mobs. Each planet class has its own creatures. Some burrow, cloak or spit acid.

Variants

  • Elites have gold names: Armored, Swift, Volatile, Regenerating, Shielded or Venomous. They appear on danger 2+ worlds.
  • Alphas are bigger, named pack leaders, one per world at a time. Killing one pays 400 credits.
  • Bosses: each class has a boss that can roam near players on danger 3+ worlds and carries a bounty.
  • Reaver raiders visit dangerous worlds and guard alien sites. On danger 3+ a Reaver Warlord can show up.
  • Lost-city guardians: precursor constructs roam lost cities, and their boss is The Last Overseer.

Creatures get tougher with danger: +22% health and +12% damage per danger level. All monsters, vanilla ones included, deal 80% of their normal damage and notice you from 80% of their normal range.

Some of the creatures

Thornback Boar, Razorwing, Bramble Lurker, Canopy Leaper, Vine Strangler, Sand Burrower, Glass Scorpion, Dune Skimmer, Dust Devil, Cliff Raptor, Rock Crusher, Frost Stalker, Shardback Crab, Snow Wisp, Glacier Tusker, Ice Shard, Spore Crawler, Fungal Shambler, Puffcap Bomber, Acid Slug, Bloat Toad, Ash Hound, Dust Strider, Rust Mite, Cinder Golem, Ember Bat, Gloom Wraith, Crater Crab, Void Leech and Shade Stalker.

Phasers hit every mob, including the custom creature models.

Alien sites and lost cities

From Frontier Wiki · where artifacts and relics come from

Alien sites

Alien sites are large points of interest: temples, stone circles, spires, crashed saucers, buried vaults, ruins and resonators. A typical planet has about 50, spread out every few hundred blocks. Earth, the space station and Nether-type worlds don't have them.

  • Finding them: the Survey Scanner (tricorder) reports the nearest site within 400 blocks. If you have an away mission or contract that needs a site, a compass on your action bar points to the nearest one within 900 blocks.
  • Discovery: the first time you walk into a site you get a title card and a bonus of 25 credits × (danger + 1).
  • Guardians: the first discovery of a site, by anyone, spawns its guardians.
  • Any site counts. Missions and contracts that say “find an alien site” accept any site on that planet, including ones you've already visited.

Site loot

Site crates always hold at least one Alien Artifact. They can also hold credits, Warp Crystals, Hull Alloy, Energy Cells, ender pearls, a data disk (about 18%) and occasionally a tool. They have a chance of a Precursor Relic: at least 8% per cache, or 2% × danger on dangerous worlds.

Lost cities

About half of shadow, ash, lunar, crystal, glacial and canyon worlds have an ancient lost city, 250 to 700 blocks from the planet's center. It's sunk into the ground and surrounded by bones and webs. Precursor constructs roam it, and its guardian boss is The Last Overseer. Defeating a lost-city guardian is a Chapter 5 campaign objective.

Asteroid fields

From Frontier Wiki · mining in vacuum

Every system has an asteroid field. Click Asteroid Field at the Helm, or run /ship asteroids. Fields are vacuum with 20% gravity and rich ore, so bring an O₂ tank and a Mining Laser. Your comm badge beams you back. The Chapter 3 quest “Rock Hoppers” asks for 20 alloy mined in asteroid fields.

Your ship

From Frontier Wiki · living aboard

Your ship is your home, your workshop and your faction. Claim your first one with /ship claim on Earth after graduating, then beam up with /ship board or right-click your comm badge.

Aboard

  • Helm: the star map. Click a system to warp. It also has Navigation, Asteroid Field and the free warp home (the corner star).
  • Ops: ship systems and contracts.
  • Transporter: step into the booth. After a 3-second countdown everyone on the pads beams down. Step off to cancel.
  • Turbolift pad: step on it to choose a deck.
  • Replicator: makes almost everything. See Replicator.
  • Cargo bay: ship storage. See Ship storage.

Departments

Size 3 and bigger hulls have every department: Bridge, Engineering (the Replicator), Science and Research (Research and Mod Benches), Med Bay and Quarters. Smaller ships fit what they can. Signs at each door name the room. The built-in stations open when you right-click them.

Protection

  • Only crew can build or use things aboard. Guests can look but not touch.
  • The original hull blocks and the outer hull can't be broken. Crew with the build right can add their own blocks inside.
  • Explosions, fire, mob griefing and liquids never touch a hull, and ships never expire.
  • Ender pearls and chorus fruit don't work aboard ships.
  • Players can't hurt each other aboard.
  • If you die, you respawn on your ship.

Renaming

/ship name <name>. Your ship's name is also your faction's name.

Cargo pod

Hauler hulls (Mule, Pelican, Crane, Titan) can beam a crew-only field cargo pod down beside the away team with /ship pod. It holds 27 slots on size 1–2 hulls, 36 on size 3–4 and 54 on size 5–6. It beams back up when the last crew member leaves the planet.

Hull classes

From Frontier Wiki · every ship you can command

There are six hull sizes. You start in size 1 and unlock each bigger size by finishing a chapter of the Precursor Signal. Refits are done at the Nexus shipyard. They're paid from the ship fund in credits plus the captain's Hull Alloy.

  • A refit can go up to one size bigger than your current hull. You can also move sideways or down, but going down costs 30%.
  • Warp time per jump grows with size, from 8–30 seconds at size 1 to 60–120 seconds at size 6.
  • When you change tier, your ship's default name updates and your interior storage moves into the new hull.

Warp and navigation

From Frontier Wiki · getting around the galaxy

Warping

At the Helm, click a system within your warp range. Warping burns fuel: 2 plus 1.5 per unit of distance. One Warp Crystal is worth 10 fuel. You can also buy fuel at Nexus Station for 2 credits per unit. The corner star on the Helm warps you home for free.

Long trips

Use Navigation at the Helm, or /ship course <earth|port|outlaw|system>. It plots a multi-jump course within your range. Shift-click Navigation to make each next jump. /ship course clear cancels it, and /ship map gives you a Galaxy Map to hold.

Raider encounters

Raiders can intercept you at warp. The chance is 25% per jump plus 8% per danger level of the route, up to 75%. When it happens the red alert sounds and everyone aboard plays the defense minigame: A/D to move, W/S for altitude, left-click to fire. Winning pays credits. If every defender is beaten, you lose 20% of your fuel. Stealth hulls are ambushed half as often.

Docking

Warp home and your ship docks at your starbase, station plot or outpost. Walk through the airlock to go aboard the base. The airlock locks again as soon as someone sets a new course.

Ship storage

From Frontier Wiki · keeping your stuff

  • Storage Controller: place one aboard for searchable storage shared by the whole crew. Right-click it to open. The title shows “Ship Storage” with used and total space.
  • Storage Modules add more space.
  • The Wireless Tablet opens your storage from anywhere on the ship.
  • Storage Crates (shulker boxes) keep their contents when broken. Chests and barrels also pack into a crate item when broken.
  • Antimatter Crates and Vaults are decorative storage furniture.

All of these are made at the Replicator. Crates you pick up on a planet and place on your ship stay yours and open normally.

Suit storage

Your space suit has pouches: open them with /suit or sneak + swap hands (F). The EVA suit has 9 slots, Pathfinder 18, Assault 27, Vanguard 36 and Precursor 54.

Comm badge

From Frontier Wiki · your lifeline

The Comm Badge never leaves you. It's locked to your hotbar and can't be put into containers.

  • Right-click: beam up to your ship when it's in orbit.
  • Sneak + right-click: emergency recall to Earth. Stand still for 10 seconds. It has a 5-minute cooldown.
  • It doesn't work while your ship is at warp, or while you're in PvP combat.

If you lose it, the Replicator makes a new one for 40 matter, or the Quartermaster sells one for 60 credits.

Tools and weapons

From Frontier Wiki · field equipment

Most Frontier tools run on Energy Cells from your inventory. One cell holds 100 charge.

Type-2 Phaser

Left- or right-click fires a beam: orange in kill mode, blue in stun mode. Sneak + right-click switches mode. Each shot uses 4 charge (3 when stunning) and deals 7 damage out to 40 blocks. Every shot wears the emitter by one condition point, out of 250. At 0 it won't fire until you repair it at the Replicator Workshop.

Type-3 Phaser Rifle

Does 12 damage out to 64 blocks for 7 charge per shot, with 400 condition.

Phaser upgrades

At the Replicator Workshop, holding the phaser, you can upgrade it up to level 5 on three tracks. The Emitter adds 15% damage per level, the Focus adds 6 blocks of range per level, and the Cells track cuts charge per shot by 10% per level. Levels 4 and up also need Alien Artifacts.

Plasma Sword

A melee weapon that does 9 damage and sets targets on fire, using 2 charge per hit. With no charge it only does 2 damage.

Mining Laser

Right-click ore at up to 20 blocks away. It uses 3 charge per block. Sneak to switch between a focused beam and a 3×3 beam. It works on planets, in asteroid fields and on Earth outside the cities. Mining data disks fit on it, and with Excavation Field the wide beam becomes 5×5.

Geo-Pick

An unbreakable pickaxe that needs no Energy Cells. It's slower than the laser but free to run. It's in the Academy kit, and the Replicator makes one for 30 matter.

Tricorder

  • Right-click: mineral scan. Ores within 20 blocks glow in their own colors, with an arrow to the nearest. It uses 8 charge. Ore names match the resource pack, so Cuperite shows as Cuperite.
  • Sneak + right-click near creatures: a life-sign scan, for 12 charge. Away missions use it.
  • It also reports the nearest alien site within 400 blocks.
  • Scans don't find ore inside the cities.

Tricorder upgrades

At the Replicator Workshop, holding the tricorder, there are three levels on each track. The Sensor array adds 8 blocks of radius per level, up to a maximum of 48. The Marker buffer adds 4 more marked finds per level, and they glow 15 seconds longer. Power cells cut charge per scan by 20% per level. The Spectral filter is a one-time fitting: once fitted, press swap-hands (F) to cycle which ore you scan for. Mk 2 costs 200 credits, 3 alloy and 1 crystal, and each level after costs more. The filter costs 600 credits, 6 alloy and 4 crystals.

Med Injector

Heals you for 25 charge, with a 10-second cooldown.

Grav Thruster

Launches you upward and forward for 12 charge.

Space suits and air

From Frontier Wiki · staying alive out there

Air

Airless worlds (Mars-type, Moon-type, Shadow, Toxic and Ash) and asteroid fields need an O₂ Air Tank in your inventory. A full tank lasts 15 minutes without a suit. A warning shows when you have 60 seconds left. With no air you take damage every 2 seconds.

Tanks refill by themselves aboard ships, at Nexus Station, at outposts, at the Academy and inside your own station plot, where air is unlimited.

Research and data disks

From Frontier Wiki · upgrading your gear

Research Bench

  • Analyze Alien Artifacts (10 data each) and Precursor Relics (150 data each) into research data.
  • Research the exact disk you want for research data plus materials.
  • Synthesize a random disk for 200 data.
  • Combine two identical disks into the next level.
  • Augment the held item, spending data and XP levels on an enchantment. Enchantments have sci-fi names, for example Sharpness is “Monomolecular Edge”.

Modification Bench

Install disks onto gear. Installing costs 2 XP levels and 50 credits per level. Merging two identical disks costs 3 XP levels. It also repairs gear: each Hull Alloy restores 25%.

Replicator

From Frontier Wiki · how everything gets made

There are no crafting tables or furnaces on Frontier. Vanilla crafting is off. Everything is made at a Replicator: the one built into your ship's Engineering department, Replicator terminals at the Academy and stations, or a Replicator Station you place yourself.

Matter

The Replicator runs on matter. Put items into the Recycle tab to break them down into matter. Recycling returns 50% of an item's matter value, so it's never a 1:1 converter. Hauler hulls hold twice as much matter and get +15% from recycling. See Matter values for what each item is worth.

Tabs

  • From matter: consumables, tools, building supplies and furniture.
  • From materials: recipes paid for with what's in your inventory, such as armor, Hull Alloy, Energy Cells and utility items.
  • Construction: building blocks, including Station Parts for station plots.
  • Refinery: ore to ingots, logs to planks and sand to glass. Pick what to refine and it's done instantly. It's free: no fuel, no matter.
  • Workshop: phaser and tricorder upgrades, and phaser repair.

Energy cells

From Frontier Wiki · power for your gear

Energy Cells power phasers, the plasma sword, the Mining Laser, the tricorder, med injectors and grav thrusters. Each cell is 100 charge. New captains start with a handful, so you'll need to make more.

Ways to get cells

  1. Field charge (no ship needed): 2 coal + 2 raw copper makes 2 cells at any Replicator terminal. Both are easy to mine in the Frontier Wilds.
  2. Carbon: 3 coal + 2 redstone makes 2 cells.
  3. Crystal: 1 Warp Crystal + 2 redstone makes 4 cells, the best rate.
  4. From matter: 20 matter makes 2 cells.
  5. Buy: 10 credits each from the Quartermaster.
  6. Rewards: orientation gives 6 cells, and site crates and loot often hold some.

The Geo-Pick mines without using any cells, so use it for bulk digging and save cells for the laser.

Away missions

From Frontier Wiki · story missions on every planet

Every planet has three away missions. Open /missions to accept up to two at once. A mission is a chain of steps, and the action bar shows a compass to your next objective.

Step types

  • Waypoint: reach a marked spot. A light pillar shows when you're within 96 blocks.
  • Recover: pick up an item at the waypoint and keep it until you return.
  • Deploy: right-click the given item at the marked site.
  • Scan: take a tricorder reading at the marked site.
  • Life-sign scan: sneak + right-click the tricorder near at least two creatures.
  • Survey: atmospheric readings at least 150 blocks apart.
  • Find an alien site: follow the compass. Any site on the planet counts, even one you've already found. Later steps then point to that site.
  • Samples, kills, deposits: collect, defeat or mine the given number.
  • Return: go back to your ship.

Mission types

Mission types include distress signals, lost probes, precursor ruins, wrecked freighters, smuggler caches, navigation beacons, relay networks, weather buoys, spill surveys, predator hunts and precursor relic vaults. Site missions only appear on planets that have alien sites, and hunts only on dangerous worlds.

Rewards

  • Credits, scaled by danger and length (base 450).
  • An Alien Artifact (25% + 8% per danger).
  • A Precursor Relic: guaranteed for “The <planet> Relic” missions, 35% for “Ruins of <planet>”, and 6% × danger on danger 4+ for the rest.
  • A data disk (12% + 2% per danger) and often Warp Crystals.

The Precursor Signal

From Frontier Wiki · the crew campaign · /story

After graduating, your crew follows the Precursor Signal: six chapters of five quests. The bar on screen shows your current quest, and /story opens the Mission Log. Progress belongs to the whole ship. Finishing chapters unlocks hull sizes, the Outlands and finally the Destiny.

Chapter 1: First Light

Unlocks size 2 hulls. Chapter reward: 2,000 credits, 15 alloy.

  1. Shakedown Cruise: dock at Nexus Station.
  2. Earning Your Keep: complete a contract and mine 8 crystal or alloy deposits.
  3. Boots on the Ground: beam down to 2 worlds and run 3 tricorder scans on planets.
  4. The Pulse: bring back an Alien Artifact.
  5. Listening Post: analyze 10 research data and complete an away mission.

Chapter 2: Echoes in the Dark

Unlocks size 3. Reward: 5,000 credits, 35 alloy, 1 data disk.

  1. A Bigger Boat: commission a size 2 hull.
  2. Fly Your Colors: name your faction, choose its symbol and pledge to an alliance.
  3. Past the Veil: chart 2 frontier sectors.
  4. Ghost Ships: salvage 3 components from wrecks and analyze 50 research data.
  5. Field Work: research a data disk, complete 2 away missions (danger 2+) and bring back 2 Alien Artifacts.

Chapter 3: Reavers

Unlocks size 4. Reward: 12,000 credits, 70 alloy.

  1. Armor Up: commission a size 3 hull.
  2. Thin Air: land on an airless world (danger 2+) and scan 2 airless worlds.
  3. Rock Hoppers: mine 20 alloy in asteroid fields.
  4. First Contact: defeat 30 hostiles (danger 2+) and drive off a raider attack.
  5. Race for the Pieces: bring back 3 Alien Artifacts and complete 2 contracts.

Chapter 4: Deep Survey

Unlocks size 5 and the Outlands. Reward: 22,000 credits, 140 alloy, 2 data disks.

  1. Heavy Metal: commission a size 4 hull.
  2. A Place to Call Home: establish a starbase.
  3. Into the Deep: chart 3 deep sectors.
  4. The Long Haul: complete 3 contracts and 2 away missions (danger 3+).
  5. The Whole Relic: combine two data disks and recover a Precursor Relic.

Chapter 5: The Gatekeepers

Unlocks size 6. Reward: 40,000 credits, 220 alloy.

  1. Capital Ship: commission a size 5 hull.
  2. Alliance Business: complete 2 away missions (danger 4+) and 3 contracts.
  3. Guardians: defeat a lost-city guardian and 20 hostiles (danger 4+).
  4. Blackmoor Haven: dock at Blackmoor Haven and scan 5 danger 4+ worlds.
  5. Resonance: recover 2 Precursor Relics.

Chapter 6: The Signal

Unlocks the Destiny. Reward: 65,000 credits, 400 alloy, 2 Precursor Relics.

  1. The Edge of the Map: commission a size 6 hull and chart 2 sectors at the galaxy's edge.
  2. Call to Arms: fight in a world siege (attack or defend).
  3. The Spires: win a world siege and complete 2 danger-5 away missions.
  4. Blackmoor Rising: fight for Blackmoor Haven, or hold it.
  5. The Answer: bring 3 Precursor Relics.

Each quest also pays credits, from 250 in Chapter 1 up to 7,000 in Chapter 6.

TipFor the relic quests, look for “The <planet> Relic” away missions. They always pay a relic.

Starbases and station plots

From Frontier Wiki · a home in space · /starbase

Starbases

Browse 40 ready-built stations with /starbase, from the Spindle at 10,000 credits up to the Citadel at 200,000. You can take a tour first with /starbase tour <id>. Warp home and your ship docks; walk through the airlock to go aboard.

  • /starbase home: go to your starbase.
  • /starbase sell <price>: list it on the pre-owned market. The minimum is 1,000.
  • /starbase market: buy a pre-owned starbase.
  • Trade-in: buying a new starbase while you own one credits 60% of the old design's price.
  • While you're docked, a scale model of your starbase shows out the bridge windows.
  • /ship invite guests can visit your starbase too.

Station plots

The first entry in /starbase is a Station Plot for 25,000 credits. It's an open 500-block-wide build zone, full world height, with a core at its center.

  • Fly freely inside it (zero-g).
  • Air is unlimited: tanks refill and you can't drown.
  • Build with Station Parts from the Replicator, made from minerals mined on planets.
  • Place a Docking Port (400 matter) where your ship should dock. Until you do, the airlock is on a small starter deck near the plot's edge, out of your way.

Outposts

From Frontier Wiki · a permanent base on the ground · /outpost

An Outpost Beacon from the Outpost Office on Nexus Station costs 2,500 credits and 40 Hull Alloy. Carrier hulls pay half. It gives you a permanent outpost in its own world that doesn't reset like planets do.

  • The build area is 193 × 193 blocks, from 48 below to 120 above the base.
  • Buy prefab kits and sci-fi furniture at the Outpost Office.
  • /outpost name <name>, /outpost public and /outpost private.
  • Air tanks refill at your outpost, and your ship can dock there.

Seizing worlds

Beam down to an Outlands world and use the claim spire at its center. Hold it for 5 minutes. You must be pledged to an alliance.

  • Everyone in the attacking and defending alliances fights, not only the crews involved.
  • The clock only runs while attackers outnumber defenders within 16 blocks of the spire. Each extra attacker adds 25% speed, up to double.
  • Attackers can step away for up to 30 seconds before the seizure fails.
  • A world that just changed hands is shielded for 15 minutes.
  • Held worlds pay 300 credits an hour in tribute, 25% more on danger-5 worlds.
  • /assist beams you into your alliance's battle, with a 2-minute cooldown.

Blackmoor Haven siege

Alliances fight for the Haven's control core, a beacon near the arrival pad. Use /haven for status, and /haven siege in the core room to attack. Hold the core room with more fighters than the defenders for 10 minutes. Afterward it's shielded for 6 hours.

The holding alliance gets 20% off outlaw gear and +10% at the fence. It also gets 3,000 credits an hour of tribute, shared by crews who fought for it in the last 7 days.

PvP and bounties

From Frontier Wiki · player combat

Where PvP is on

PvP is only on danger-5 worlds and in the Outlands. Everywhere else (ships, stations, outposts, the Academy, other planets) it's off. Crewmates can never hurt each other.

Rules

  • If a player kills you in a PvP zone, everything you carry drops and the killer takes 20% of your wallet, up to 50,000.
  • A PvP hit tags both players for 15 seconds. While tagged you can't beam out or use escape commands such as /spawn, /home, /tpa or /warp.
  • Logging out while tagged drops your gear to whoever hit you last.
  • PvP worlds pay 1.5× on contracts and exotic samples, and keep landing sites at least 250 blocks apart.

Bounties

Use /bounty place <player> <amount> (minimum 100, plus a 5% posting fee) and /bounty to see the board. /bounty cancel <player> withdraws yours with a 75% refund. Whoever kills the target in a PvP zone collects the bounty.

Frequently asked questions

From Frontier Wiki · quick answers

How do I get more Energy Cells?
At any Replicator terminal, 2 coal + 2 raw copper makes 2 cells. 1 Warp Crystal + 2 redstone makes 4. 20 matter makes 2. The Quartermaster sells them for 10 credits each. See Energy cells.
How do I smelt ore? There are no furnaces.
Use the Refinery tab of any Replicator. Choose what to refine and it's done instantly. It's free: no fuel and no matter.
Where do I mine before I have a ship?
Take the Wilds Line from the station on the south side of Frontier City to the Frontier Wilds camp. You can't dig inside the cities.
How do I get Precursor Relics?
“The <planet> Relic” away missions always pay one. “Ruins of <planet>” missions pay one 35% of the time. Alien-site crates have at least an 8% chance. See Alien sites.
My mission says “Find an alien site” but nothing happens.
Follow the compass on your action bar to the nearest site and walk into it. Any site on that planet counts, even one you've already visited. If it says no site is in range, explore further out.
How do I upgrade my ship?
Finish chapters of the campaign (/story) to unlock bigger sizes. Then refit at the Nexus Station shipyard using the ship fund and Hull Alloy. You can only go up one size at a time.
How do I add a friend to my crew?
/ship crew add <name>. They have to have graduated from the Academy first. To let someone visit without joining, use /ship invite <name>.
Where do I sell things?
Only at the Station Exchange on Nexus Station. Use /ship course port to get there.
My stuff on a planet disappeared.
Planet worlds regenerate every 72 hours. Build on your ship, a starbase, a station plot or an outpost instead.
I'm stuck or lost on a planet.
Right-click your comm badge to beam back to your ship. If your ship isn't in orbit, sneak + right-click it and stand still for 10 seconds to recall to Earth.
I'm running out of air.
Carry an O₂ Air Tank on airless worlds. Tanks refill aboard ships, at stations, outposts and the Academy. A full space suit makes air last longer, and the Precursor Exosuit needs none.
I lost my comm badge / tricorder / field manual.
Badge: 40 matter at the Replicator or 60 credits from the Quartermaster. Tricorder: 140 matter or 150 credits. Field manual: /guide.
Why can't I break blocks on my ship?
The original hull is protected. You can only break blocks you or your crew placed, and you need the build right (Captain, First and Second Officer, Chief Engineer).
Can I use ender pearls to get out of a ship?
No. Pearls and chorus fruit are blocked aboard ships.
Why do my items show “◆ RARE” and so on?
That's the item's rarity. See Items and rarity.
Where is PvP allowed?
Only on danger-5 worlds and in the Outlands. See PvP and bounties.
What happens if I die?
You respawn on your ship. In a PvP zone, if a player kills you, you drop everything you carry and lose 20% of your wallet to them.
How do I get a bigger suit pouch?
Wear a better suit. EVA has 9 slots, Pathfinder 18, Assault 27, Vanguard 36 and Precursor 54.
What is the fastest money early on?
Take contracts on nearby worlds, sell Alien Artifacts (140 each) and Relics (1,800), and mine deposits. Pledging to the Federation adds 20% to contract pay, and the Syndicate adds 15% to sales.
